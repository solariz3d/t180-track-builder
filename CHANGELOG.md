# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **The wordmark reads "Track Builder".** The second word takes a capital B again; the rest of the sentence-case copy from 0.4.27 is unchanged (D512; the user, 2026-10-28).
- **Number fields have arrows in each look's colours.** The up and down arrows on every number field were the plain white Windows box in every look; they are now two small chevrons drawn in the look's own colours, brighter under the pointer, and clicking or holding them still steps the value. The fields keep their size.

### Fixed

- **Level and To floor work with a turn typed as a corner.** With "turn & climb as" on corner (the default), a turn in the turn field made Level and To floor refuse and place nothing, with the reason only in the message line. They now place the turn exactly, as Extend would, and still end the piece level (and To floor on the floor). A climb typed as a corner is still refused by name, since the climb is what they solve.

## [0.4.27] - 2026-10-28

### Added

- **The right-button look has no wall.** Dragging the view with the right button now locks the pointer to the preview for the drag, so the mouse never reaches the edge of the screen or another monitor; the look keeps going as long as the button is held, and the cursor comes back where it was at the release. If the runtime refuses the lock, the drag works as before and stops at the edge.

### Changed

- **The piece handles sit at its front, middle and back.** Width and cupping are now at the back of the new piece, bank and climb in its middle, and length and turn stay at the front, so the controls no longer crowd one end.
- **Labels read as words, not shouting.** Section headings, the camera bar and its caption, the readout over the 3D view, the Export layouts dialog and the wordmark are in sentence case without wide letter spacing ("Equation track", "Build", "length", "T-180 Track builder"), and Settings ▸ Theme lists "Sigil" like the others. A track with no name is "Untitled" (it said "unsaved"; the dot after the name is what marks changes not yet saved). The left panel's summary ends "loop open" instead of "open · last extend 0 ms" (a timing for developers), and the checker's line no longer repeats that the loop is open.

### Fixed

- **No white window before the builder opens full screen.** Opening the builder first showed a small white window where it used to open, and only then went full screen. The window now stays hidden until the page has drawn itself, and appears once, full screen, in its own colours.

## [0.4.26] - 2026-10-27

### Fixed
- **Tab moves on from a button you just clicked.** After a click a button keeps the focus, and Tab there switched Sculpt on or off instead of moving the focus to the next control. Tab now switches Sculpt only when nothing is focused or the 3D view is; on a button, a link or a menu heading it moves the focus as usual.
- **The live warning for low room overhead goes away when the road above is moved.** When a road that passed less than 1 m over another was raised or moved, the amber "low overhead" warning could stay on the lower road until something else made the whole track check again, if that lower road also ran close beside a third road. It now clears as soon as the road above moves away.
- **On a closed lap that ends on a jump, Sculpt marks the right piece at the start line.** Sculpt hatches the last and first road pieces of a closed lap, the ones a release may reshape. When the lap's last piece was a jump's flight, that hatch landed on the flight and showed nothing; it now marks the last road piece before it.
- **Sculpt's Grab glyph no longer stays turned after you leave the window with Alt held.** Alt released while the builder was not focused never reached it, so the Grab glyph stayed turned until the pointer next moved. It now resets when the window loses focus.

## [0.4.25] - 2026-10-27

### Changed
- **Reverted: the Aurora sky is back to its first look, the one it had up to 0.4.9, at the author's judgment in the app.** Two curtains, a green one and a higher, fainter violet one, hang round the sky and fold slowly, with fine rays, over a field of fine stars; the stars stay a pixel across at any screen size, as they were from 0.4.9. The redrawn sky of 0.4.24 (curtains from horizon to horizon, the 3D corona, the slow colour drift, and its GPU check with a flat fallback) and the three-curtain sky with a corona that came before it are both taken out. The 0.4.24 section below still says what that release shipped. Everything else in 0.4.24 stays: the export's CSP warning and the close button's hover on Aqua and Glass.
- **The builder opens maximized.** It fills the screen when it starts, with the taskbar still showing, and the minimise, maximise and close buttons work as before.

## [0.4.24] - 2026-10-27

### Changed
- **The Aurora look has a new sky: real curtains, with depth.** The old sky hung three rings of light round the horizon, and looking up showed a flat starburst that was brightest and green at its centre, the wrong way round. Now the curtains run from one horizon over the sky to the other, as real aurora arcs do, with slow folds and curls and rays running along the lower edge. The nearest curtain is drawn in 3D, so looking up its rays converge with real depth: green at their bases, faint at the top, the way a corona looks from underneath. Over about ten minutes the red tops and pink lower edges fade and swell, while the green stays. The large shapes still drift slowly enough to stay behind the work, nothing is drawn below about 10 degrees, and with reduced motion the sky holds still in the skin's own colours.
- **The sky checks that it fits the graphics card.** The new sky costs at most 1.5 ms of GPU time a frame at 2560×1440 on the machine it was built on. For its first few seconds the 3D view times the sky on the GPU. If it is over 1.5 ms a frame, the view switches to a flat form of the same sky, about a quarter of the cost, and says so in the console. Where the browser offers no GPU timer, the sky is drawn as it is.

### Fixed
- **The export's CSP warning no longer points to a setting that is not there.** Since 0.4.20, every export said the track needs Custom Shaders Patch and then told you to turn off "T-180 track" to export without it, but the builder has no such setting: every track it exports needs CSP. The warning now says only that the track needs CSP and that Assetto Corsa without it can crash loading the track.
- **The title bar's close button turns red under the pointer on the Aqua and Glass looks.** Those two looks cleared every window button's background and gave the hover back only to minimise and maximise, so the close button stayed plain when hovered. It now turns the same red it does on Aurora.

## [0.4.23] - 2026-10-27

### Fixed
- **Stretching a piece with an edge typed on an empty track keeps up with a 144 Hz screen.** With only a left or right edge angle typed and nothing placed, a 1,000 m stretch still took about 9 ms a move at the slow end, over one frame at 144 Hz: on each move the builder re-read the edge ramp's curve samples from scratch and worked out the road's edge cross-section again at every chord end. It now keeps the samples for as long as the ramp's shape allows and works out each distinct cross-section once a piece, so the same drag takes about 6.5 ms. The road, the files and the 3D view are exactly as before.
- **Dragging a piece with an edge typed no longer slows every frame.** With a left or right edge angle typed and nothing placed yet, the 3D view worked out the road's width at the ghost's end again on every frame, about 1.5 ms each time, while a handle was dragged; it now takes a quicker exact route to the same number. Each move of the handle also wrote out and compared the whole cross-section of every part of the ghost, about 10 kB each; it now skips the parts that cannot have changed, and the ghost is built exactly as before. Turning a 1,000 m piece with an edge after a placed piece now costs about 4.4 ms a move, within one frame at 144 Hz (it was about 8.7 ms). Stretching one on an empty track costs about 9 ms at its slowest moments (it was about 10): the rest is the edge's own fit, still to come.

## [0.4.22] - 2026-10-27

### Added
- **Track ▸ Bank the lap, for a lap that is already closed.** Since 0.4.19, closing a loop banks its turns, but a lap closed before then kept its flat turns, and no command banked a lap that was already closed. Bank the lap banks every upright curve of a closed lap into its turn, as closing a loop does, and leaves the route exactly where it is. It is one undo step, and it is greyed out until the lap is closed. The status line says which pieces it banked and how far, and names any piece it held back: a loop or a steep ramp (too steep to bank), a piece that would lean out of its turn, or a place where the turn changes direction faster than the road can roll.

### Fixed
- **Dragging a long piece with bank, cup or edge typed is smooth again.** Since 0.4.20, stretching or turning a piece while any of those fields held a value refitted the whole ramp in a way whose cost grew with the cube of the piece's length: a 1,000 m stretch redrew about 4 times a second instead of every frame, and a 10 km piece froze the builder for over two minutes per move. The fit now works only where each control point reaches, so its cost grows in step with the length (10 km: 10 to 23 ms, from over two and a half minutes). The road it lays is the same: on every test track and saved track it was checked on, every bank, cup and edge value came out identical except one, on one piece, which lay exactly halfway between two values at the ninth decimal place and rounded to the other one.
- **Bank the lap pressed again leaves the lap as the first press made it.** A second press used to add a little more bank to a piece the first press had banked by only a few degrees (a run-out next to a loop, for one), and each press added an undo step. A lap that already carries its bank is now read as banked: the status line says "nothing to bank: every curve on this lap already has its bank; nothing changed", and nothing is added to the undo history. A bank you laid yourself, even one of a few degrees on a straight, is never taken away by it.
- **Bank the lap names every loop it leaves flat.** On a lap with a loop whose turn is gentle, the status line left that loop out of "no bank on …: it climbs or dives too steeply to bank". It now names every piece the loop rule holds back that would otherwise have been banked.
- **Bank the lap keeps a copy of the track from before.** As Smooth ramps does, it writes the track as it was to the app's track-backups folder before it changes anything, so the lap before the bank is there after the session's undo history is gone. If that copy cannot be written, the lap is not banked and the message says so.

## [0.4.21] - 2026-10-26

### Changed
- **A Bend, Climb or Stretch on a closed lap keeps all of what you did when it safely can.** When you let go, the builder closes the lap again. Away from the start line it used to do that partly by reshaping the very road you had just changed, so a quarter to half of your bend could quietly go. It now first tries closing the lap with the road at the start instead. It takes that only when the start road moves no further than your own drag moved the track, the lap still has a straight long enough for the start, and no new red appears; then your change is kept whole, the start road that moved is hatched, and the message says "to keep your bend, the start of the lap moved". Otherwise it closes the lap as before and says how much of your bend that took back. Right at the start line it still tells you plainly when your bend had to be undone.
- **The bank that Finish adds is now smooth all the way through its joints.** When Finish closes a lap it banks the turns it lays, and 0.4.20 left a small kink in that bank where one piece met the next and where the closer met the start line: the lean itself was continuous, but how fast it was changing was not, which the outer wheels feel as a tap. The added bank now flows through every joint without that step, with the same limits on how fast and how suddenly it may roll, and the same refusal when a closer would lean out of a turn; a turn keeps its lean right to its exit, and the last of the roll-off now finishes on the first stretch of the straight after it instead of exactly at the joint. Close's banking of a whole lap gets the same. Saved tracks are not changed; Track ▸ Smooth ramps is for those.

### Fixed
- **Close and Finish say when the bank's smoothing stopped short.** On a lap with tight turns the banking's smoothing can run out of room before it is quite finished; it used to stop quietly. The report now says so in plain words, for example "the bank on the lap stopped about 5% past its smoothness limit at one point (p4)", so what the road carries is never a silent default.
- **Closing a lap whose start is a flat cup takes the start piece into account again.** On such a track (TEST 10 among them) the Close button's first try, which lets the start piece and the last piece share the bend, was quietly refused twice over: once on a rounding whisker below zero in the cup, once because keeping the cup's curvature smooth across the start line would have pushed the cup below zero. The whisker now counts as zero, and where the cup cannot stay smooth across the start line without going below zero the close keeps it merely continuous there and says so in its report, the way Finish already does for a cup that must come home over a short closer.
- **A track whose edge was still rising or falling at its end can be extended again.** On a track from a file (or after a cut) whose last piece ends with its edge angle still changing, Extend refused every new piece with a message about the joint instead of adding it. The new piece now carries the edge on as it was going; only a piece that truly cannot stay inside the edge's limits is refused, and then it says why.

## [0.4.20] - 2026-10-25

### Added
- **A warning where bank, cup and edge stack in a turn.** A turn that is banked, cupped and has its edge raised on the banked-up side can ride rough at speed: on a test track the car clipped the road and went unstable at high speed and load in such turns, and the same turns with the edges at 0 rode fine. The track checker now shows an amber line, "bank, cup and edge stack here and the turn may ride rough at speed", with the stretch it covers. It fires where the road's slope changes by more than 8° across the car's 2 m width on the raised side and all three are there; nothing is refused, capped or changed, and the edges work as before. It is a warning to use them with care.
- **Track ▸ Smooth ramps, for tracks laid before this version.** A saved track keeps the ramps it was laid with, so the smoother banks, cups and edges below reached it only where a piece was laid again. Smooth ramps re-lays every bank, cup and edge change on the open track the new way, so each one starts and ends without a jolt; the route stays exactly where it was (every turn, climb, width and length is untouched), and so do the values at every joint. On TEST 10 it re-lays 13 ramps on 9 pieces, and the 19 places where the road's curvature stepped at a joint go to none. It copies the track to its backups first and is one undo step; it never runs by itself, and on a track with no ramps, or one already smooth, it changes nothing and says so.

### Changed
- **Banks, cups and edges now arrive and leave smoothly.** A bank change, a cup from → to or an edge from → to used to reach its new value by a curve whose slope was zero at both ends but whose curvature was not, so under the wheels the road's vertical curvature stepped all at once where the ramp began and where it ended: on TEST 10 that was 15 °/100 m at every 30° bank joint (1.8 g at 300 km/h), 20 where the 35° right edge begins and 10 where the closer brought the cup home, the bump that stayed after the ridge was gone. The ramp is now a curve whose curvature is also zero at both ends, so a joint into or out of a ramp carries no step. Ends and lengths are the same, and a ramp now reaches the value you typed exactly at its transition and holds it (a fitted ramp used to end a hair off, which once let a closer's bank drift 15° along its new road); a ramp is a little steeper through its middle (1.875 times the average instead of 1.5), so a roll the builder sizes for the roll-rate warning comes out longer. Close brings the cup home the same way where the closer is long enough for it, and by the slowest straight fade where it is not, as before; and closing a loop now keeps the join at the start line, and every joint it adjusts, as smooth as the rest (it used to match the road's angle and slope there, not its curvature, so a closed lap kept one such step at the start line). Tracks already saved keep the ramps they were laid with until a piece is laid again.
- **The README, the first-start card and the guide say what a first-time user needs, and match 0.4.19.** A newcomer following them hit three walls. Nothing said the exported tracks need Custom Shaders Patch, though the export's own `surfaces.ini` warns that plain Assetto Corsa can crash loading it. The guide's step 2 sent them to Build ▸ Brush, which is gone. The README described a Close with a preview and Apply, a Sculpt that never moves the road, and no pit lane. Now the README's first screen and Install section, and the first-start Assetto Corsa card, say "Requires Custom Shaders Patch (CSP)". Guide step 2 reads "Press Tab (or Build ▸ Sculpt mode), pick a tool with 1–9, drag on the road", and step 1 is two sentences. The README describes one-press Close, Sculpt mode with its nine tools and keys (Bend, Climb and Stretch move the route), and the pit lane as it is. It drops "a pit lane" from "Not built" and names the installer as the release names it, `T-180.Track.Builder_0.4.19_x64-setup.exe`. A guide-text test no longer forbids the word "sculpt", which was the old piece builder's word and is now this builder's own mode. Tests: `app/test/core-eqonly.test.js`, `app/test/onboarding.test.js`, `app/test/share-install.test.js`.
- **An export's warnings are in plain words.** The message after File ▸ Export… used to show notes meant for the builders: source file names, design-document sections, a mesh-naming detail you could do nothing about. It now says what you can act on, for example that the track needs Custom Shaders Patch (CSP) and that Assetto Corsa without it can crash loading the track. Export to Assetto Corsa says the same after it writes the track. The exported folder is the same as before.
- **Sculpt's how-to line is no longer red.** The line under the tool strip that says what the tool does, and the value line beside it, were drawn in the colour the builder uses for errors. They are instructions, so they now use the normal text colour.

### Fixed
- **No more ridge across the road where one piece's cross-section meets another's.** Where two pieces met with a different number of points across the road, as happens where a cup deepens or an edge begins, the exported track stitched the two together by the order of their points instead of by where they sit across the road. On a cupped or edged road that stood a thin wall of road straight up out of the surface, across its width: up to 44 cm tall on one test lap. In the game it showed as a streaky ridge across the road and was felt as a bump. The stitch now joins points that sit side by side, so it lies in the road, and the export drops any part of a stitch that still faces along the road instead of up out of it. The builder's preview drew the same wall, which is why its centre line broke at that joint; the line is now continuous. Before writing a track, the export now checks the road it is about to write and refuses, in plain words, if any such wall is left, instead of letting you find it by driving into it. Re-export a track to get the smooth road in the game. The cause and the rule are in docs/SEAMS.md.
- **A Bend, Climb or Stretch that closing the lap takes back now says so.** On a closed lap, letting go of a route tool closes the lap again. Next to the start line the only road that can close it is the road you just changed, so your bend was quietly undone and the message only said the lap was closed. It now says "your bend was undone to close the lap; bend further from the start, or open the lap first", and when closing takes back part of a change it says how much.
- **The result of Export to Assetto Corsa shows in full.** Beside the button it was cut to "ex…". The whole message, including where the track was written, now also shows across the top of the window until you close it with ✕.

## [0.4.19] - 2026-10-25

### Added
- **SIGIL has a melody of its own, Obsidian.** Choosing Settings ▸ Theme ▸ SIGIL used to keep a melody picked at random, because SIGIL had none. It now plays Obsidian: a slow D minor tune with a dark flat second, one short figure repeating while the chords move under it, on a soft Phantoms pad (a new pair of place and undo sounds, levelled with the others). Random now picks among five looks and five melodies, SIGIL and Obsidian among them. A saved theme loads as before.
- **A warning where a road passes less than 1 m over another.** Roads closer than 2 m are no longer red unless they run into each other, so a road could pass so low over another that a car on the lower one would hit it, with nothing said. Less than 1 m of room under another road is now a yellow warning, "another road passes less than 1 m over this one here". It warns; it does not stop an export. Road that runs into road is still red.
- **Two new ways to reshape placed road: Stretch and Smooth.** Both are tools in the new Sculpt mode (below). **Stretch** makes a piece, or a run of pieces, longer or shorter, and the road after it slides along with its end. By default the piece keeps its bend: a 90° corner stretched to half again its length is still a 90° corner, only wider, and it still turns and climbs at its two ends exactly as it did where it meets the pieces either side. Asked to keep the rate instead, it keeps how hard the road turns per metre, so a longer corner turns further. Because both ends are kept as they were, a big stretch leaves a corner turning a little harder at its two ends than in its middle (by about a tenth at half again its length). A very short piece, one with fewer than five control points, has no middle to rescale, so it keeps its rate rather than its bend: a 20 m corner stretched to 40 m turns twice as far. A jump inside a run is left as it is, a piece is never made shorter than 1 m, and on a closed lap the stretch leaves the gap it opens for the lap to be closed again when you let go. **Smooth** irons a wobble out of one setting of the road (its turn, climb, bank, width, height and so on) under a brush: each control point is drawn toward the average of its neighbours, most at the brush's centre and not at all at its edge. The road outside the brush is left exactly as it was, the brush's outermost points are held so it blends in, and a joint between two pieces inside the brush is smoothed across like any other point. A stronger smooth runs more passes, so it never overshoots. (`src/core/sculpt.js` `stretch`, `smooth`.) Test: `test/core_sculpt_stretch.test.js`.
- **Sculpt is now a mode of its own.** Build ▸ Sculpt mode, or Tab, switches the builder into it and back. Its tools are named by what your hand does to the road, not by the setting they change, and the first nine sit on the keys 1 to 9 (1 Grab, 2 Stretch, 3 Bend, 4 Climb, 5 Twist, 6 Width, 7 Cup, 8 Edge, 9 Smooth). [ and ] narrow and widen the brush, and while you drag the mouse wheel does the same. The brush strip's on box and its mode and channel lists are gone: picking a tool picks all three.
- **What each Sculpt tool does to the road.** Grab lifts or sinks the road under the brush (with Alt, moves it sideways) and leaves the road after it where it was. Stretch, Bend and Climb change the route itself, so the road after them moves too. Twist, Width, Cup, Edge, Wall rise and Tube sweep change only the road's shape and never move the line you drive along. Smooth irons a wobble out of the road under the brush. A tool that moves the route on a closed lap closes the lap again when you let go: first only across the stretch you brushed, then with one more road piece either side, then the whole lap; if none of those closes it, the drag is undone and the builder says why. The brush can act on a run of pieces you have selected instead of a radius, a handle on a run sets the value you ask for where the handles read it, and Esc drops a drag and puts the road back. (`app/core/coreshell.js`.) Test: `app/test/sculpt-tools.test.js`.
- **Sculpt shows on the road what each tool will do, before you press.** With the pointer over the road in Sculpt mode, the stretch the brush can reach is shown in cyan with a mark at each end (for Stretch, the whole piece under the pointer), and a handle in the tool's colour, the colour Build uses for the same setting, shows which way to drag and what each way does. Bend, Climb and Stretch also tint the road after the brush, the part that will move; on a closed lap the last piece before the start line, and the first after it, are hatched, because letting go may move them to close the lap again. While you drag, the change at the centre is shown by the handle, in the strip under the bar and, large, over the 3D view. The reach is now worked out once per drag rather than on every frame, which pays for the new marks.

### Changed
- **Closing a loop banks every curve on it that needs it, not only the new road.** When you press Close, every flat curve on the closed lap is now banked into its turn by the same amount Finish uses (three quarters of the bank that balances the car at the design speed), easing in and out with the curve, including when the end already meets the start and no new road is laid. A curve you banked or rolled yourself keeps your bank exactly, and so do loops, steep ramps, tubes, straights next to a curve, and the road beside a pit lane. A curve laid as several pieces now banks as one curve. Where a curve turns into one the other way sooner than its bank can roll across, the bank eases to none between them and Close says where; a curve that unwinds gradually into the next keeps its bank, and so does a corner laid with a short transition, all the way to its exit. On a long track the view redraws only the road Close banked or laid, not the whole loop. The whole change is part of the one Close step, so one undo takes it back.
- **Two roads closer than 2 m are no longer red, unless they actually run into each other.** A road could not pass within 2 m of another part of the track, beside it or over it, even where the two never touched, so a bridge low over the road, a lane close beside it or a tight corner on a wide road was refused. Now only road that runs into road is red, as "the road passes through itself"; the 2 m measure is still worked out, as information. The checker that runs as you build, Close and the radius limit had only the 2 m rule to see a road running into itself, so the checker gained a test of its own: whether the other road crosses this one, at each station and between stations. Export and the overlap check of a preview build the track itself and refuse wherever the road meets itself, as before.
- **Finish to start now banks the curves it makes, and joins the track smoothly.** The road Finish adds to close a lap was laid flat, so its curves leaned the wrong way (on one 27 km test lap, 453 places leaned out and the side load reached 10.7 g at the design speed). Each new curve is now banked into the turn by three quarters of the bank that would balance the car at the design speed, easing in and out with the curve. Its bank also starts and stops gently at every joint, so there is no bump where the new pieces meet each other or the track (the change of the road's curve under the outer wheels was up to 96 °/100 m; it is now at most 15, within the track's own joints). The cup is brought from the end's value to the start's over the whole new road, at about 0.01 °/m, instead of in the last 30 to 40 m before the start line. Where the end must roll upright first, that roll is now laid long enough to start and stop gently, so the new road can be a little longer (on that lap 3,990 m instead of 3,864).
- **Finish to start no longer leans its roll the wrong way, and picks the better order when the end must roll upright.** When the end of the track is banked, Finish rolls it upright on a straight of its own, either first or as the last piece onto the start. Laid first, that straight could be bent into a curve while it was still rolling, so the road leaned out of the bend (by up to 26° on one test lap). It is now kept straight. Finish also used to take the roll-last road whenever it held, even where it left the new turns on the end's own bank, unbanked, with up to twice the side load (10.9 g against 4.7 g). It now tries both and keeps the one with the lower side load at the design speed. The cup also now changes at one even rate over the whole new road, instead of faster at its start and end.
- **Finish to start never lays new road that leans out of its curves.** On some tracks, the road that closed the lap kept the bank of the track's end through turns that went the other way, so it leaned out of its curves the whole way round. Finish now checks every road it could lay and never chooses one that leans out of a curve by more than 5°. If every road that would close the lap leans out, it says so instead of laying one.
- **A brush drag draws only the stretch it is changing.** While the brush was dragged, every step rebuilt the preview of the whole track at a coarser detail, and a closed lap was rebuilt in full each time: about 25 ms a step on a 12-piece track and 60 to 120 ms on a long lap, measured in node. Now the placed road stays as it is during the drag. The stretch under the brush is drawn from a few dozen longer chords instead of thousands of 2 m ones, and when a bend or a climb moves the road after it, that road is moved as one piece instead of being built again. On a closed lap the lap is drawn open while the drag lasts, with a line across the gap. The exact road is built in the frame after you let go, never while the pointer is released, and the drag's last picture stays on screen until it is ready, so nothing blinks. Nothing exported changes: the export and the checks read the exact track, as before. (`app/preview/trackmodel.js`, `app/preview/coarse.js`, `app/preview/preview.js`.) Tests: `app/test/sculpt-drag-model.test.js`, `app/test/preview-speed.test.js` row 4, and `app/test/sculpt-frame.test.js` rows 1 to 4 for the frame times.
- **After a bend, a climb or a stretch on a closed lap, only the road near the change and the end of the lap move.** Letting go of such a drag closes the lap again. When the brushed stretch alone could not close it, the whole lap was adjusted, so 47 to 49 of the 50 pieces of a 10 km lap shifted slightly. The builder now first tries the brushed stretch together with the last piece before the start, then with the first piece too, and adjusts the whole lap only if neither closes it: after a bend at 2 km, 5 pieces move instead of 47. The release takes about as long as before. (`app/core/coreshell.js`.) Tests: `app/test/sculpt-tools.test.js` rows 6, 20 and 21.
- **The Aurora sky looks more like a real aurora.** It now has three curtains at different depths (a tall bright near one, a middle one, a lower, finer and fainter far one) instead of two. Each is striped by fine rays with real gaps between them, and a strong ray stands taller.   across the whole width and stop at the edge. The old 8 m walls rose past that edge, the rims that flipped up. A wall
  can still be sculpted on any word.
- **The bank ramps.** A word banks no faster than real T-180 roads do (0.849°/m, the fastest tenth of the library's
  banking, measured by `tools/bankrate.cjs`). A word that would bank faster is made longer at its radius, so it turns
  further. Before, a wall-ride after a turn banked 52° inside 36 m, and validation marked the seam sharper than measured.
- The tempos now set a piece's size from the library's corners: standard is the median corner, compact a small one,
  grand a large one. The older aurora and serpents stay, so tracks that use them still open.
- The bowl hairpin, the S and the spiral climb name their turn angles, so they stay a hairpin, an S and a spiral at the
  new sizes.
- Tracks made before this change open and build with the same centreline: a document stores every handle of every
  word, so only words placed from now on get the new defaults. A bowl, half-pipe or flat word in an old track keeps its
  wall and gets the measured floor under it.
- **Flying is per axis.** W/S, A/D and Q/E each move on their own, so W and D together go diagonally, at the same speed as a
  straight line. If both keys of one pair are held, the newer one wins, and the other takes over when it is released.
  Before, only the last key you pressed moved.

### Added

- **A piece can take its new width (or bank, or cup) in a short ramp at its start and hold it.** Extending after another piece blends a width toward its target over the whole piece; the user wanted the whole piece at the new width without a bottleneck at its start. Each field can now be set to ease in over the whole piece (as before) or to reach its target within about one knot span (at most 20 m) and hold it for the rest of the piece; the joint into the piece is still smooth (a later piece cannot jump), and the ramp cannot overshoot its target or ring. The first piece of a track already holds what was typed from its start.
- The palette suggests what usually comes next, from how often each kind of word follows another on the real tracks.
  It only suggests: every word can still be placed after any other.
- **Cup: the road's cross-section deepens toward a half-pipe, apart from bank.** A new channel sets the angle the walls have
  turned by at the edges, from 0 (flat) to 150 (a partial tube): about 15 is the old bowl, 31 the old half-pipe, 90 vertical
  walls. It blends along the piece like the others, moves neither the line nor the bank, and rolls with bank (bank 30 with cup 60
  stands the left wall vertical). Tracks made before this open and render exactly as they did: a cup is only there when you set one.
  Closing a lap whose cup meets an uncupped start holds the cup at the start's edge and fades it into the start's shape over the last 10 m,
  or refuses by name if it cannot; the validation panel reds any cup joint or lap seam that steps more than 1 mm.

### Fixed

- **Closing the window with unsaved changes now asks first:** Save, Don't save, or Cancel. Before, the X closed at once and
  the unsaved track was gone (the pieces builder kept an autosave; the equation builder kept nothing). Save does what the
  Save button does; if the track has no name yet the window stays open with the name box focused, so nothing is lost.
  Cancel, or closing the prompt, keeps the window open. With nothing unsaved the X closes at once, as before. If the
  prompt itself cannot be shown, the window still closes: the X never stops working (`app/closer.js`, D192's rule).
- **Exported tracks now work in Assetto Corsa: the car stays on the road, and the road is lit.** Found in the first drive
  of an exported track in AC with CSP (2026-10-02, a 6 km oval). Two causes, both in the kn5 the export writes:
  - The car fell through the road. Every road cell sat under its own transformed node and reused its piece's mesh name
    (3,001 of 3,003 road meshes transformed; 9 distinct names for 3,003 meshes). AC drew that, but its physics did not
    collide with it. The export now writes every mesh in world space under identity nodes, with a unique name
    (`src/export/acready.js`). A copy with only that change drove at over 900 km/h. The preview keeps its per-cell
    transforms, so sculpting is as fast as before. Not yet split: whether AC needs the flattening, the unique names, or
    both.
  - The road rendered black under any light. Its `ksPerPixel` material had no `txDiffuse` texture, and that shader takes
    its colour from one. Every material without a diffuse now gets a small solid one (grey for the road, near-white for
    the paint), and the preview draws the same textures, so the preview and the kn5 still list the same materials.
  - A bump at 900+ km/h where one piece meets the next, felt in the drive and seen in its replay. It is not a step: the two
    edges meet within 0.5 mm. The builder bridged that half-millimetre with a strip of slivers (about 1e-4 m², thinner
    than 4 mm) whose normals face along the road, so a tyre that landed on one met a wall. The worst crease the car
    crossed on the lap was 167° there and 0.34° anywhere else. The export now moves the later piece's edge onto the
    earlier one wherever they are within 2 mm, and drops every seam triangle thinner than 4 mm. Seam triangles that bridge
    a real gap (a change of cross-section across part of the width) are kept. On the oval, the worst crossed crease is
    now 0.34° everywhere, measured along the driven line; whether the bump is gone in the car is still to be driven.
  - To adapt: re-export a track to get the fix. A track exported before this still falls through.
- **The speed the builder checks a track at now goes to 970 km/h** (it stopped at 764). 764 was the 99th-percentile
  speed of seven laps on tracks whose straights never let the Mach 6 reach top speed. The exported oval let it: p99 970,
  max 971 km/h, with 57% of the lap above 764 (docs/FINDINGS.md §3e). The lap check and the speed slider both use the
  new cap. Loads go with speed squared, so a fast track can now show amber or red in corners that read green before,
  because the check finally counts the speed the car reaches.
  - Known, not changed here: the sealed fixture digests (D190 row 5) no longer match for the two closed-loop fixtures,
    because their kn5 bytes changed on purpose. They need re-sealing.
- The preview on an empty track now draws and answers the wheel, keys and mouse at once, looking at where the first piece will start. Before, with nothing placed and no ghost, the view did not draw at all, and input given meanwhile was applied late, when the Extend ghost first appeared: the "lag until the first piece is put down" (D195).
- A piece whose width (or rise rate) changes is drawn smoothly. Each 2 m slice of it used to be drawn at its own middle width, so a width change was a staircase of steps between slices, and at the start of a piece that eases into a new width the road stepped by up to a quarter of a metre; every slice now runs from its own start shape to its own end shape, so neighbouring slices meet. Roads whose width does not change are drawn exactly as before. This changes how three saved test tracks are drawn (a width change, a brushed rise rate, mixed families) and nothing else.
- The first piece of a track is now what you typed along its whole length. Before, a width, bank, turn, climb or cup typed for the
  first piece started at the default and grew to your value by its far end, so every new track began with a bottleneck (31 m
  widening to what you asked). The fields you leave as shown keep their default start; pieces after the first still ease from
  where the track is to what you type.
- The window's X no longer does nothing. After switching between the equation and piece builders (which reloads the page) the close request was left waiting on a handler that no longer existed, so the X, and Alt+F4, did nothing in the equation builder; a failing autosave could hold the piece builder open the same way. One close handler is now registered for both builders, cleans up (the piece builder still clears or saves its autosave), and always closes the window, even if the cleanup fails or hangs.
- The cup's migration-fixture test no longer fails on a fresh Windows checkout: git's line-ending conversion (core.autocrlf, on by default there) rewrote the hashed fixture files to CRLF, so every digest read "FIXTURE FILE CHANGED". A .gitattributes now keeps test/fixtures exactly as committed.
- Holding a movement key and pressing C or B no longer snaps the camera back to free on the key’s auto-repeat.
- Flying forward or back together with up or down no longer slows to a crawl as the view looks down (it was 0.9× at the
  starting view and 0.01× straight down). Every key combination flies at the same speed, in any direction you look.
- A sideways trackpad swipe no longer zooms the view, or changes the lens with Ctrl. Shift+wheel on a device that reports
  it as deltaX still works (×4).
- The label of the piece at the build head is always drawn, even when that piece is off-screen, and label boxes are opaque
  (no border), so their text stays at least 4.5:1 over bright road.

- Export from the app: an Export… button writes the closed track as an Assetto Corsa track folder into a folder picked
  in the native dialog.
  - It runs the same exporter as the command line (`src/export/fromwords.js`) inside the window. The output is
    byte-identical to it, apart from PNG compression.
  - A red track is refused and its reds are listed.
  - Inside an AC install only `t180b_*` folders are ever written, and another track's folder is refused, by the page
    and again natively. Nothing is installed or launched.
- Close the loop from the palette: it appends the safest connector as one undo step, since a track must be a closed lap
  before it exports.
- Autosave and crash recovery: an unsaved track is written to the app's data folder shortly after each edit, and the
  next start offers to restore it or discard it. Nothing is written inside the repository.
- A design-speed picker in the validation panel, starting at 460 km/h: the median speed of seven clean Mach 6 laps
  (`docs/FINDINGS.md` §3d).
  - Every word without its own speed is validated at it, so the load colours appear while building: information at the
    20 g suspension stop, amber above the proven 90 g.
  - It edits no document.
- `tools/speed.cjs`: speed percentiles and propulsive acceleration per speed band, measured from replays.
  `docs/FINDINGS.md` §3d records the measurements with their command and sample. No replay bytes are in this
  repository.
- A window proof (`scripts/prove_render.js`, `scripts/prove_render.ps1`). It starts the built app, places a real track
  through the palette, and captures the app's own window (PrintWindow, never the screen) in the build, overhead, chase
  and free views. It checks that the track is drawn, that the build view looks along the growth direction, and that the
  head is in frame. The captures stay outside the repository.
- A jump now carries its landing ramp: after the flight, a straight stretch of road at the landing pitch, long enough
  that the car touches down on it at both measured falls (3.2 g and 6.3 g) with a margin. It is sized by validation's
  own `landingRamp()` in `src/validate/jumps.js`. The build head sits on road after a jump, and the next piece starts on
  the landing, blending from the ramp's cross-section like any other join.
- The ghost of the next piece: pointing at a piece in the palette shows it, see-through, at the head, exactly as a click
  would place it. Leaving hides it.
- The preview reads as a track:
  - lit surfaces, so floors, banks and walls differ;
  - white edge and centre lines, and a tie across the road every 10 m;
  - a faint ground grid at height 0, which is editor UI, not scenery;
  - a marker at the build head that stays readable from any distance.
  The overhead camera frames the whole track.
- A load graph in the validation panel: the hardest line's load along the track, with the 20 g and 90 g limits and the
  red and amber stretches shaded. It is hidden while there is no load.
- Validation:
  - an open track's head must sit on road; a head in a jump's flight is red (`head-in-the-air`);
  - at a known take-off speed, a landing the road does not catch is red (`landing-misses-zone`).
- A headless end-to-end test (`test/doc-e2e.test.js`): words become a closed loop, which is exported with every check
  on and read back with every start, pit and timing marker.
- Sculpt handles (`app/handles/`): every handle of a placed word, with its physics bounds.
  - A drag stops at the red bound (known breakage, which the export refuses) and passes amber (unproven, never
    blocked).
  - Each drag is one undo step. Alt goes past red and shows it.
- Race markers placed by the program (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5c), in `src/markers/` and a markers panel
  (`app/markers/`, built and tested headless; not yet mounted in the page):
  - every marker sits in track coordinates, anchored to a word and a distance into it, on the surface the mesh builds,
    so it moves with its word when earlier words are edited and stands correctly on banked floors and half-pipe walls;
  - the grid comes from a pattern (2 staggered, 2 abreast, 3 abreast) with count, spacing and slot-by-slot edits,
    numbered from pole;
  - pit boxes, with `pitboxes` counted from them; they can stand on a pit lane once one is built;
  - the hotlap start sits at the run-up that reaches the design speed at the line, from the measured thrust;
  - start/finish and sector gates stand at the edges of the cross-section;
  - the start/finish line, grid boxes and pit boxes are painted as surface meshes generated from the markers, so paint
    and markers cannot disagree;
  - §5c's red checks (start line ahead of the grid, L/R gates, height and heading, slot overlap and off-road, pit
    count, a marker whose word is gone) refuse the export.
- `exportTrack` takes a marker layout (`opts.markers`). Without one it places the default layout on the longest
  straight, where it did before.
- Texture slots in the document (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5b, part 1): floor, walls, lines, kerbs and edge glow. A font
  carries defaults and any word can override them (schema 2); older documents and piece libraries migrate on load and
  resolve to the same track.
- Texture tools in `src/texture/`, not yet used by the mesh, the preview or the export:
  - mapping by each lateral line's own arc length and the profile's own width, so a texture does not stretch through
    tight turns, walls or loops (the mesh's current centreline mapping is off by up to 650% on a 15 m turn);
  - PNG and baseline JPEG import, converted to uncompressed DDS with a full mip chain; progressive JPEG, interlaced PNG,
    images over 8192 px and a compressed normal map are refused with a named reason;
  - one texture set feeding both sides: the preview reads its images back from the same DDS bytes the kn5 embeds.
- A texture panel (`app/texture/`), built and tested headless; not yet mounted in the page.
- A procedural texture maker (`src/texmaker/`, `app/texmaker/`): asphalt grain, noise, gradient bands, stripes, lane
  lines, panels and seams, emissive glow strips, and stamped decals, each a parameter set. A texture is canonical text
  that regenerates byte-exact at any resolution; `makeTexture(text, w, h)` gives the RGBA pixels the texture tools turn
  into DDS. Four presets: asphalt, rainbow, lanes, neon-night. The editor panel is built and tested headless; not yet
  mounted in the page.
- A pit lane in the document (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §11.1): a side road anchored to the loop at a leave and a rejoin
  point, easing out to its own run beside the road and back in, with no step and a continuous tangent at both joins.
  Setting, editing and removing it are one undo step each. The document is now schema 3; schema-1 and schema-2
  documents migrate on load. Not yet editable in the app.
- The pit lane in the export: its own drivable cells, the pit boxes placed on it, and its crossings with the road
  refused as red, with the self-intersection check on. No AI pit line (`ai/pit_lane.ai`) is written yet, so AI cars
  cannot pit.
- Projects of several layouts, each its own document, exported as one track folder with one set of files per layout
  (`models_<layout>.ini`, `<layout>/`, `ui/<layout>/`), laid out the way AC's installed multi-layout tracks are.
- A texture slot can hold a texture made in the program: its parameter text, rendered at a chosen size (a power of two,
  16 to 8192) in both the preview and the export, so a track's look is text that regenerates byte-exact. A "Make…"
  button per slot opens the texture maker.
- Texture packs: one shareable file with a font's full look and the images it uses. Importing refuses a name clash, a

- A starter phrasebook (`src/doc/phrasebook.js`, ARCHITECTURE §11.7): step flow, bowl hairpin, S and spiral climb.

  lines it comes from. They are in the palette (below).
- Shareable codes (one line of text) for a track, a piece or phrase, and a texture pack. They import byte-exact, and a

- "Install to AC": pick the Assetto Corsa folder once (remembered in the app's own data folder), and the track is
  exported straight into `content\tracks` as `t180b_<name>`. It never writes over a folder the builder did not make.

  random edits (`scripts/soak.js`; 60 ops in the default suite, 10,000 behind `T180_SOAK`) checking round-trips,
  byte-identical undo, save/open and the preview against a full build, and tests that an edit rebuilds only the cells
  it touches and that no mesh crosses to the native side during a drag. The benchmark shows every edit on a 40 km track

### Changed

- `.gitignore`: `out/` added (the build's output folder).
- Both platform-test folders now carry the same `ai/fast_lane.ai`.
- The document's quanta are finer (0.1 mm, 0.00001°), so a closed loop can meet its start within the geometry's
  tolerance. The canonical text changes accordingly.
- Validation's steep check (red above 50° without CSP) now measures the actual surface normal at every station and
  lateral line, so a banked road counts as well as a steep wall.
- `npm test` now also runs the app's headless tests (`app/test/*.test.js`).
- The lap sim runs at the measured full-thrust acceleration, a table against speed: about 25 m/s² below 400 km/h and
  11.5 m/s² at 700–800. So a closed track's lap proof runs with no speed given. With the default car it runs only on a
  closed loop; an open track without a design speed claims no load.
- Backspace alone no longer removes the head; Ctrl+Backspace does, so a stray key cannot delete track.
- The default hotlap start is no longer 12 m behind the pit boxes. It is where the run-up reaches the design speed at
  the line (352 m at 460 km/h), which on a short loop wraps back round the track.
- The document's canonical text now carries each word's `textures` (schema 2) and the document's `pitLane` (schema 3).
- The geometry and app mutation harnesses run their child test processes at `--test-concurrency=4`, so a full-suite
  run no longer fans each child out across every core.
- The texture-memory budget reports per texture, per slot and in total. It turns amber above the median and red above
  the largest embedded texture load of the eleven T-180 tracks measured (56.3 MB and 328.9 MB of kn5 file bytes),
  replacing an inferred 256 MiB line. Red is shown, not a refusal.
- The texture decoder takes a size cap, so a small hostile code cannot demand a large buffer.
- README: the status is no longer "planned, not built". A table judges every item of ARCHITECTURE §1–§6, §5b and §5c
  as tested, built or not yet, naming the file and the test.
- The track's frame is the yaw-pitch (gravity) frame plus the word's roll, instead of a rotation-minimising frame, so a
  word's roll is its bank against gravity. Before, a climbing turn silently banked the road (61° over a full turn at
  10° pitch). This is a deviation from ARCHITECTURE §3, recorded in `docs/INTERFACES.md` §2. Old documents keep their
  centreline; on pitched turns their bank changes to what the words say.
- Sculpting a word moves everything after it rigidly, unless the edit changes the pitch the next piece starts at. The
  pieces after it are re-placed, not regrown or re-meshed, and the path stays bit-identical to a full rebuild.
- The 3D preview builds its path at the export's station step (2 m), so it shows the stations the kn5 is built from,
  and it publishes that one path (`t180:track`) for validation instead of each keeping its own.
- Validation reads the preview's path and re-checks only from the changed word on, plus the stations within stacking
  reach (at most 34 m with the built-in words) of what moved. The stacking check measures only pairs of stations that
  could be within 2 m, with the same findings bit for bit. A closed loop's lap proof waits for the end of a drag.

### Removed

- The app's "skip self-intersection check" switch, and its pass-through to the exporter. The check is always on in the
  app. A track it refuses shows its reds; a false red is fixed in the geometry, never by switching the check off.

### Fixed

- `scripts/build_platform_test.js` no longer continues when `src/export/scene.js` cannot be loaded: validation fails
  loudly instead of being skipped.
- Built meshes no longer repeat node names. A word resolves into up to three segments that share its id, and
  `src/geom/mesh.js` named cells by the id alone, so the names repeated (16 of 31 nodes on a five-word track). That also
  made the self-intersection check report crossings on tracks that do not cross, which refused every default export.
  Pieces are now named by id and part.
- The palette no longer shows "nullnull" under the track list.
- The window no longer scrolls: the panels scroll inside themselves.
- A panel that fails to start (the preview, the camera, validation, handles) now says why in its own area.
- The built app now carries `tools/`, which the exporter needs. The in-window module loader no longer breaks on a file
  that declares a name it also provides.
- The preview canvas follows devicePixelRatio (sharp on scaled displays) and resizes with it.
- Ctrl, Alt and Meta shortcuts no longer switch the camera (Ctrl+C is copy).
- The lap sim's speed cap was 745 km/h, read from the speed at track B's hardest moment rather than a top speed, and
  both track B laps run above it. It is now the measured p99, 764 km/h (`docs/FINDINGS.md` §3d).
- The self-intersection check pairs each mesh with its own cell by position, never by name. A repeated name can no
  longer make one pass of the road "meet" itself. Real crossings and stacks are still found.
- The jump counter read "0 jumps" with a jump placed, because it counted drawn landing arcs, which leave out a jump
  still waiting for its landing. It now counts every jump and names the ones waiting.
- Validation measured every jump one station step too long (from the station before the lip), so a 12 m gap read as 13
  m, and the 6.3 g landing seemed to need 311 km/h instead of 287.
- The right-hand panel no longer scrolls sideways: wide content (the load graph) is held to the panel's width.
- The marker check read a 3-abreast grid as pointing 40° off the road. It took the race direction from the back slot to
  pole, which crosses the road when they stand in different columns; it now removes the across part, and a grid
  pointing backwards is still refused.
- An edit that would leave the track unable to resolve (for example an end roll the next word cannot follow) is refused
  with a message and changes nothing, as a drag frame is; before, it was committed with a hidden resolve error. Found
  by the soak.
- The colour levels no longer scan every red and amber range for every station on every edit.
- The soak script sculpts only top-level words, so a starter phrase in the palette no longer crashes it.
- The app mutation harness copies `tools/` as well as `src/`, so the export's kn5 read-back works in its temp copies.
